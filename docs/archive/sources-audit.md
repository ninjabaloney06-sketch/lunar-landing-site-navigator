How to read the table:
- **Link:** `ok` = HTTP 200 directly. `redirected` = the server redirected (only a trailing slash here). `dead` = would mean it did not load (none).

- **Coordinates / frame:** exactly as printed in the source. "—" = the source gives none.

## Fix suggestions for Leo

1. **Row 1, wrong quote attribution.** In the 2022 section, replace "communication capabilities with Earth" with the 2022 wording "ease of communications with Earth" (criteria listed as "terrain slope, ease of communications with Earth, and lighting conditions"). Keep "communication capabilities with Earth" only for the 2024 release. The 2022 page could also be cited for region size: "Each region is approximately ... 15 by 15 kilometers", sites have "an approximate ... 100-meter radius" (image caption, same page).
2. **Row 3, LPSC column order is settled.** Replace "(S to confirm against the abstract's figure)" with: confirmed by the Figure 1 caption "Nobile Rim 2 (-83.95°S, 58.74°E)", so latitude is printed first despite the "Cen. Lon. Cen. Lat." header. Also call the values **mosaic centres** ("Cen." in Table 1 of "ten combined ... controlled mosaics"), not official NASA region centres. Connecting Ridge + Extension, de Gerlache Rim 1 + 2, and Nobile Rim 1 + Leibnitz Beta Plateau share one mosaic each but still get separate centre pairs.
3. **Row 3, projection and LOLA quotes are slightly off.** The "[IAU2000:30120]" sentence is about the ShadowCam images, which were "map projected at 1 meter/pixel in the Moon south polar stereographic projection [IAU2000:30120;9]" to match the NAC mosaics. The LOLA part reads: images were "manually shifted ... to hillshade maps made from the LOLA 5 meter/pixel DTMs", with positional accuracy "within 5 meters of LOLA". The current "controlled to 'the LOLA 5 meter/pixel DTMs ...'" condenses this; quote it as above. The abstract does **not** name the frame of the Table 1 lat/lon. Leo to decide whether to treat them as planetocentric, east-positive (the usual LROC/LOLA convention). No source in this list says so for this table.
4. **Row 9, add the VIPER frame.** It is the only source that names one: "Moon (2015) - Sphere / Ocentric", radius 1 737 400 m, IAU 30100:2015 (Appendix B, PROJ `+proj=stere +lon_0=31.6218 +lat_0=-85.42088 +R=1737400 +units=m`). Note that the paper writes lon before lat ("31.6218°E, 85.42088°S"). Optionally add the Appendix A primary-area polygon.
5. **Row 8, replace the registry with the primary source.** [LROC "IM-2 Athena Seen Obliquely", published 10 Mar 2025](https://lroc.im-ldi.com/images/1408): "ended up on its side within a 20-meter diameter crater (84.7906° S, 29.1957° E)". Same numbers, correct hemisphere. Supporting NASA text: [NASA release 7 Mar 2025](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-receives-some-data-before-intuitive-machines-ends-lunar-mission/), "more than 1,300 feet (400 meters) from its intended landing site". Arithmetic cross-check: intended vs actual differ by about 0.32 km in latitude and 0.18 km in longitude (≈ 0.37 km total, with the intended site rounded to 0.01°), so consistent. Then drop the For All Moonkind entry (all rights reserved, not NASA).
6. **Rows 7, 12, LROC license note: make it precise.** The footer says "All rights reserved", but the [LROC Terms & Citation page](https://lroc.im-ldi.com/about/terms) is what governs reuse. Website images (Featured Images etc.) "are copyrighted". Use "for educational purposes is encouraged and permitted without prior permission", with the credit "NASA/GSFC/Arizona State University". Commercial use needs permission. PDS-delivered data "are in the public domain". For Space Apps (educational, Apache 2.0 code) this probably means: cite with that credit line, and prefer PDS products for any image in the app. Leo/Isaac to confirm.
7. **Rows 11, 13, VIPER status is outdated; use NASA primary sources.** [NASA Ends VIPER Project, 17 Jul 2024](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-ends-viper-project-continues-moon-exploration/): "its intent to discontinue development of its VIPER ... project", citing "cost increases, delays to the launch date, and the risks of future cost growth". Then [NASA Selects Blue Origin to Deliver VIPER, 19 Sep 2025](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-selects-blue-origin-to-deliver-viper-rover-to-moons-south-pole/): "Blue Origin will deliver VIPER to the lunar surface in late 2027". It is a task order (CS-7) whose delivery is an **option** NASA has yet to exercise. That release gives no landing site, so whether the Mons Mouton site (Beyer et al.) still applies is **unverified**. Change the heading from "rover cancelled" to e.g. "cancelled 2024, delivery re-awarded 2025". Drop the Wikipedia citations, or keep them only as "secondary". Remove the Griffin/FLIP row unless a primary source with a site turns up.
8. **Row 12, add IM-1's actual landing site.** [NASA "NASA's LRO Images Intuitive Machines' Odysseus Lander", 26 Feb 2024](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/lro/nasas-lro-images-intuitive-machines-odysseus-lander/): "Odysseus came to rest at 80.13 degrees south latitude, 1.44 degrees east longitude", elevation 2,579 m, "local terrain is sloped at 12 degrees". Keep 80.2°S, 1.0°E labelled as the pre-landing nominal area.
9. **Row 2, AI-use rule from the same NASA guidelines page** (matters for our AI-built submission): "attribution of the information directly to NASA is not permitted" for information incorporated into an LLM. NASA Insignia may not "appear with AI generated imagery". NASA "strongly encourages AI-generated products maintain a marking". Worth one line in sources.md and in the submission's AI-use section.
10. **Row 5, label our own conclusion.** Rewrite "the candidate-region set above remains the reference list" as "(our assumption, not stated by NASA)". Or drop it.
11. **Row 6, the "Shackleton–de Gerlache ridge = Connecting Ridge" identity has no source.** Either cite Beyer et al. 2025 ("the connecting ridge near Shackleton", one of VIPER's three 2020 candidate areas) and word it as "the ridge connecting Shackleton and de Gerlache craters, listed by NASA as Connecting Ridge (identification ours)", or find a NASA/LROC page that states it.
12. **Row 10, add a license line for SVS 4937.** NASA media guidelines apply, except the video's soundtrack ("Music Provided by Universal Production Music"), which is third-party. Do not reuse the video audio.
13. **Row 3, for Leo's physics check.** Nobile Rim 2's mosaic centre (−83.95°) is 6.05° from the pole, just outside NASA's "within six degrees of latitude". Probably harmless (regions are ~15 km across, the value is a mosaic centre), but worth a note when the sites are built.

Nothing in sources.md was found to be fabricated. Every number quoted there appears in its cited source, except where noted (row 1 quote, rows 5–6 own conclusions).
