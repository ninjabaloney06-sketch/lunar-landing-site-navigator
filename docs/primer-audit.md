## Summary for Leo

- **Two sign errors.** At the *south* pole, the elevation of the Sun (or Earth) above a flat horizon is **minus** the subsolar (or sub-Earth) latitude, not "equal" to it (rows 5 and 13). The text that follows row 13 already uses the correct sign.
- **Four claims have no source:** "a few tens of meters of relief" (row 6), the DTE line-of-sight paragraph (row 15), "the LTE demo flown on IM-2" (row 18), and "tens of kilometers" of ray length (row 19). Barker et al. 2023 actually trace horizons out to **310 km**.
- **The ME–PA offset is overstated as a per-site shift.** 875 m is the total rotation angle expressed as a distance on the surface. Close to the south pole, the pole-tilt part dominates, so a site moves by **about 0.66 km (derived)**, not about 0.9 km (row 26).
- **One open number can now be filled in.** The DE440 ME frame differs from the DE421 ME frame by about **53.4 cm** (2000–2040), according to the frame kernel itself (row 30).
- **The primer never mentions `IAU_MOON`.** IAU_MOON is a third lunar frame and SPICE's *default* body-fixed frame for the Moon. It differs from MOON_ME by up to about 155 m (rows 24 and 28). See the draft frame note below.

## Draft frame note (for Leo)

*Draft for S. Based only on primary sources: the NAIF frame kernel and PCK comments, the LOLA/PGDA product pages, Barker et al. 2023 and the LRO/LGCWG coordinate white paper. Numbers marked **(derived)** are arithmetic on the cited values.*

**Three lunar body-fixed frames exist in SPICE.**

- **MOON_PA** (principal axes) has its axes along the Moon's principal axes of inertia. This is the frame JPL's lunar orientation data are given in: the binary PCK `moon_pa_de440_200625.bpc` "provides the orientation of the lunar Principal Axes (PA) reference frame" ([PCK comments](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/generic_kernels/pck/moon_pa_de440_200625.cmt)). PA is used for dynamics, gravity and laser ranging ([LRO white paper §6.2](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/luncoordwhitepaper-10-08.pdf)).
- **MOON_ME** (mean Earth / polar axis) has +Z along the mean rotation axis, with the prime meridian through the mean Earth direction ([frame kernel](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/generic_kernels/fk/satellites/moon_de440_250416.tf)). In SPICE it is a *constant* rotation away from PA. For DE440 that rotation is (67.8526″, 78.6944″, 0.2785″) about axes 3, 2, 1. The current MOON_ME is an alias of `MOON_ME_DE440_ME421`, which was built to match DE421's ME frame to about 53.4 cm for 2000–2040 (same kernel).
- **IAU_MOON** is the mean-Earth frame computed from the IAU/IAG report's rotation formulas, supplied in the generic text PCK (`pck00011.tpc`). The IAU formula cuts off its libration series, so it differs from the DE-based MOON_ME by up to about 0.0051° (≈ 155 m) for 2000–2040 (frame kernel). Unless told otherwise, **SPICE uses IAU_MOON as the Moon's body-fixed frame** (frame kernel, "Lunar body-fixed frame associations"). Our code must therefore always name `MOON_ME` explicitly.

**IAU vs. mean Earth.** These are not two competing systems. Both the IAU/IAG Working Group and the LRO project use the mean Earth/polar axis *system*. The white paper says ME use "is consistent with the recommendations of the IAU/IAG Working Group". The difference is in the *realization*: IAU_MOON uses a truncated analytic formula, while MOON_ME is derived from a JPL ephemeris (DE421 for LRO). LRO decided: "the Mean Earth / Polar Axis (ME) lunar reference system shall be used" for archival products, targeting and "inter-mission communications", with planetocentric latitude/longitude (white paper §9). PA is only for "specific applications internal to the SOCs".

**What our sources use.**

- **LOLA south-pole products:** MOON_ME of DE421. Barker et al. 2023 say the polar products are "centered at the pole in the MOON_ME frame of the JPL ephemeris DE421". [PGDA 90](https://pgda.gsfc.nasa.gov/products/90) says the same: "in the MOON_ME reference frame of the JPL DE421 ephemeris". Heights are relative to a 1737.4 km sphere, and the stereographic projection is true scale at the pole ([PGDA 69](https://pgda.gsfc.nasa.gov/products/69)). The only PA product is the global [PGDA 95](https://pgda.gsfc.nasa.gov/products/95) ("in MOON_PA DE421 frame"), made for geophysics, which we don't need.
- **Site coordinates in `docs/sources.md`:** *none of the site sources names ME or PA explicitly*. Indirect evidence:
  - The Artemis region centres ([LPSC 2024 #1775](https://www.hou.usra.edu/meetings/lpsc2024/pdf/1775.pdf)) come from mosaics controlled "to the LOLA reference frame" and projected in "IAU2000:30120" (a south polar stereographic projection on the IAU 2000 Moon sphere).
  - VIPER ([Beyer et al. 2025](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/PSJ/ae061a)) uses SPICE and a local stereographic projection on "Moon (2015) - Sphere / Ocentric", which is planetocentric.
  - The LROC IM-1 and IM-2 pages give bare latitude/longitude pairs.
  - All of these are LRO-based, and LRO delivers ME-only planetocentric coordinates (white paper §6.1). So **ME of DE421 is the likely frame, but this is inferred, not stated**. S should confirm, or ask a mentor at the event. The ME–PA difference near the pole is about 0.7 km (below), which is larger than a 20 m DEM pixel, so this matters.

**Size of the ME–PA offset.** The total rotation is ≈ 0.02886° ≈ 875 m along a great circle (frame kernel). Check **(derived)**: √(67.85² + 78.69² + 0.28²) = 103.9″ = 0.02886°, × 1737.4 km = 875 m. The white paper rounds this to "about 1 km at the lunar surface". How far a given point moves depends on where it sits. **(derived)** Near the south pole the 78.69″ tilt dominates: ≈ 0.66 km. The 67.85″ spin term adds only about 0.57 km × cos(latitude), i.e. ≈ 0.05 km at 85° S. For comparison, the DE421 vs. DE440 ME difference (53 cm) and the IAU_MOON vs. MOON_ME difference (≤ 155 m) are smaller.

**Recommendation (S decides).**

1. **Sites, DEM, horizon mask, map:** all in **MOON_ME**, planetocentric latitude/longitude on the 1737.4 km sphere. These are the frames our LOLA products and (very likely) our site coordinates already use. No conversion is needed anywhere in the terrain chain.
2. **Ephemeris:** load `de440s.bsp`, `moon_pa_de440_200625.bpc` and `moon_de440_250416.tf`. Ask SPICE for the Sun and Earth vectors from the site **directly in `'MOON_ME'`**. The frame kernel does the PA→ME rotation, as its own example `SPKEZR('Earth', ET, 'MOON_ME', …)` shows. Then build the local east-north-up frame at the site from its MOON_ME latitude/longitude.
3. **Never use** `IAU_MOON`. It is the SPICE default wherever no frame is named: the legacy routines `SUBSOL`/`SUBPT`/`ET2LST` and the dynamic-frame subsystem, according to the frame kernel. The newer `subslr`/`subpnt`/`sincpt` take the frame as an argument, so pass `'MOON_ME'`. Also never move latitude/longitude values from one frame to another without rotating them. To convert a PA point to ME, rotate its body-fixed vector with `pxform('MOON_PA', 'MOON_ME', et)`, the same call PGDA 95 uses in the other direction. Do not rescale latitude/longitude numbers directly.
4. **Validation at the event:** before comparing with JPL Horizons, find out which lunar frame Horizons uses for the observer site. Otherwise a 0.7 km site offset will look like an ephemeris error. *(Not checked in this audit; for S's Horizons learning task.)*
