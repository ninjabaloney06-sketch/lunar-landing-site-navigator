# South-pole site sources (Phase 1 reading list)

Primary sources for current CLPS and Artemis south-pole sites. Reading list only — the site data file (`data/sites.json`) is built at the event, not before. All URLs checked to load (HTTP 200) on 24 Oct 2026; coordinates are quoted **exactly as written in the source**. Nothing here is fabricated; what could not be verified is labeled **(unverified)**.

## Artemis III candidate regions (13 regions, 2022)

**Source:** [NASA news release, 19 Aug 2022 (last updated 8 Jan 2024)](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-identifies-candidate-regions-for-landing-next-americans-on-moon) — names the 13 regions (no coordinates on the page): Faustini Rim A · Peak Near Shackleton · Connecting Ridge · Connecting Ridge Extension · de Gerlache Rim 1 · de Gerlache Rim 2 · de Gerlache-Kocher Massif · Haworth · Malapert Massif · Leibnitz Beta Plateau · Nobile Rim 1 · Nobile Rim 2 · Amundsen Rim, all "within six degrees of latitude of the lunar South Pole". Quoted facts: "All 13 regions contain sites that provide continuous access to sunlight throughout a 6.5-day period"; selection criteria include "communication capabilities with Earth" and lighting.
**License/usage:** no explicit license text on the page; NASA media content "generally ... not subject to copyright in the United States", reuse for educational/informational purposes with NASA acknowledged as source, no implied endorsement, NASA insignia/logotype protected ([NASA Images and Media guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/)).

**Region center coordinates (as quoted):** [Collins et al., LPSC 2024 abstract #1775, LROC team, ASU](https://www.hou.usra.edu/meetings/lpsc2024/pdf/1775.pdf), Table 1. The table's printed header reads "Cen. Lon. Cen. Lat." but the value pairs are unmistakably (latitude, longitude) — longitudes such as 124.68 or −136.68 cannot be latitudes (S to confirm against the abstract's figure):

**License/usage (LPSC abstract):** no license stated on the document; standard practice is citation of authors + LPSC abstract number (USRA/LPI-hosted). The abstract also states the 1 m/pixel mosaics are "map projected ... in the Moon south polar stereographic projection [IAU2000:30120]" and were controlled to "the LOLA 5 meter/pixel DTMs of the corresponding landing sites".

**Update, nine regions:** [NASA news release, 28 Oct 2024](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-provides-update-on-artemis-iii-moon-landing-regions) — updated set "in no priority order": "Peak near Cabeus B", "Haworth", "Malapert Massif", "Mons Mouton Plateau", "Mons Mouton", "Nobile Rim 1", "Nobile Rim 2", "de Gerlache Rim 2", "Slater Plain" (no coordinates given). Same license situation as above.

**Status note (checked 24 Oct 2026):** NASA's [Artemis III mission page](https://www.nasa.gov/mission/artemis-iii/) (last updated 19 Oct 2026) now describes Artemis III as "a series of objectives in low Earth orbit" with the crewed landing moving to Artemis IV — the candidate-region set above remains the reference list of south-pole landing regions.

## Shackleton–de Gerlache ridge (= NASA's "Connecting Ridge")

The ridge between Shackleton and de Gerlache craters appears in NASA's 2022 list as **Connecting Ridge** (and **Connecting Ridge Extension**). Coordinates as quoted in [LPSC 2024 #1775, Table 1](https://www.hou.usra.edu/meetings/lpsc2024/pdf/1775.pdf): Connecting Ridge **−89.46** (lat), **−136.68** (lon); Connecting Ridge Extension **−88.99**, **−104.47**. NASA's own pages list the region by name only (link above). License as for the LPSC abstract.

## IM-2 (Intuitive Machines, "Athena"; landed 6 Mar 2025)

**Intended site, as quoted:** "The Intuitive Machines IM-2 intended landing site (-84.78°, 29.13°E)" — [LROC featured image "IM-2 Landing Region", 26 Feb 2025](https://lroc.im-ldi.com/images/1401); same page: landing "on Mons Mouton on 6 March 2025", "160 kilometers from the south pole".
**Actual touchdown (third-party registry, not NASA):** "Latitude: 84.7906° N"/"Longitude: 29.1957° E" (the page text describes the site as *south*; the N in their coordinate field is inconsistent) and "Landing Date: 6 March 2025, 17:30 UT"; lander "resting on its side, unable to generate sufficient power" — [For All Moonkind Moon Registry, IM-2](https://moonregistry.forallmoonkind.org/im-2/). **License:** "©2023 For All Moonkind — All rights reserved." Use as background only, verify before citing in the submission.
**License (LROC):** "©2026 NASA/GSFC/LROC. School of Earth and Space Exploration, Arizona State University. All rights reserved." — stated on the page; this is stricter than generic NASA media guidance, so LROC images need explicit credit and a rights check before reuse in the app.

## VIPER / Mons Mouton (rover cancelled; site analysis published)

**Site, as quoted:** "The mission area ... is approximately 5 × 4 km and centered at **31.6218°E, 85.42088°S**", "on the southern edge of Mons Mouton (previously Leibnitz β)", northwest of Nobile crater — [Beyer et al. 2025, "VIPER Site Analysis", Planet. Sci. J. 6, 236](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/PSJ/ae061a). **License:** "Original content from this work may be used under the terms of the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence" (CC BY 4.0).
**Earlier announcement (2021, Nobile rim):** NASA SVS visualization "The VIPER Landing Site" — rover to explore "an area west of Nobile crater, near the lunar South Pole", ~36 square miles, no coordinates — [SVS 4937, 20 Sep 2021](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4937).
**Status (secondary source):** "NASA terminated the VIPER project in July 2024 due to cost increases and launch delays." — [Wikipedia, VIPER (rover)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VIPER_(rover)), CC BY-SA 4.0. **(primary NASA release URL not located in this pass — verify in phase 2)**. Same article: NASA "still plans to support the Griffin lander to arrive on the Moon in fall of 2025", now carrying Astrolab's FLIP rover instead of VIPER **(secondary, verify)**.
