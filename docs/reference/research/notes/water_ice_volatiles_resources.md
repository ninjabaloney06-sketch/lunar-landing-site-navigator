# Water ice, other volatiles, PSRs and in-situ resources at the lunar south pole (evidence for landing/base site ranking)

Research date: 2026-11-14. Coordinates are decimal degrees, latitude negative = south, longitude east-positive (-180..180) unless a 0-360 value is explicitly quoted from the source. Evidence classes used throughout: **[DIRECT]** = direct detection of H2O/volatiles; **[INDIRECT]** = hydrogen (neutrons), temperature/illumination models, albedo or radar proxies; **[MODEL]** = geological/thermal modelling; **[SPEC]** = speculation/press characterisation.

Source-quality note: several NASA/NTRS PDFs (LCROSS Science paper, McClanahan 2024, Barker 2023, VIPER slides, LWIMS TM, NASA ISRU plans, MSolo results, Hayne 2021) were text-extracted locally and read directly; numbers from those are quoted from the documents. Crater centre coordinates/diameters marked "WP" come from Wikipedia infoboxes (which cite the IAU/USGS Gazetteer) because the Gazetteer search page could only be read for A-C entries.

## 1. What does each dataset say, and where?

### Takeaway
Only one measurement is a ground-truth direct detection of bulk water at the south pole: LCROSS in Cabeus (5.6 +/- 2.9 wt%, 2009). Everything else is either a surface-only optical signal (M3, LAMP, LOLA: patchy, <= few % by mass in most places, mutually inconsistent), a ~1 m-deep hydrogen proxy at tens-of-km resolution (LPNS, LEND: ~0.2-0.9 wt% WEH in the big PSRs), ambiguous radar, or thermal modelling; the newest optical result (ShadowCam, Science Advances, March 2026) finds no widespread surface ice above ~20-30 wt% and only a few 20-50 m candidate patches. No landed mission has yet measured ice in situ at the south pole as of November 2026 (PRIME-1 lasted ~12 h on its side; VIPER now late 2027; Chang'e-7 slipped to 2027; Lunar Trailblazer lost).

#### 1.4 Documented disputes between datasets

- LEND resolution: Lawrence et al. 2011 (Science technical comment) argued the CSETN count rate is dominated by uncollimated high-energy epithermal neutrons; Eke et al. 2012 found ~1 % most likely fraction of detected neutrons that are lunar and collimated, with more than half the count rate from cosmic rays striking the spacecraft; Teodoro et al. concluded the collimated flux "is negligible". The LEND team claims roughly one-third of signal is collimated. NASA's own ISRU study labels the ~10 km CSETN resolution "controversial" - [Teodoro et al. arXiv:1304.8123](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1304.8123); [Lawrence et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1203341); [Air & Space/Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/air-space-magazine/a-scientific-dispute-165960447/); [LWIMS TM](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20205008626/downloads/TM-20205008626.pdf).
- NSRs outside PSRs: LEND reported neutron suppression not coinciding with permanent shadow (not predicted by the standard cold-trap model); the 2024 LEND paper instead argues suppression scales with PSR area - [review](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06056v2); [McClanahan 2024](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2303.03911).
- Surface vs buried: "there is currently no strong correlation between the two types of data sets (surface vs. buried reservoirs)" - NASA LWIMS; LAMP, LOLA and M3 surface maps are mutually inconsistent and do not match the LEND distribution - [LWIMS TM](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20205008626/downloads/TM-20205008626.pdf); [review](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06056v2).
- "Heterogeneity problem": LEND shows high H at the young (~0.9 Ga) LCROSS PSR but no excess in the oldest PSR (Shackleton), opposite to the age-accumulation expectation - [review](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06056v2).
- Concentration scale mismatch: LCROSS 5.6 +/- 2.9 wt% vs neutron 0.2-0.9 wt% WEH; reconciled only by sub-footprint patchiness (30-40 % areal fill) or a dry cover layer - [LWIMS TM](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20205008626/downloads/TM-20205008626.pdf).
- M3 (up to ~30 wt% in some pixels) vs ShadowCam (no widespread > 20-30 wt%; LROC team: "no albedo patterns indicative of ice, frost, or lag deposits" in Shoemaker or any other PSR craters) - [Li 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/6130389); [SOEST 2026](https://www.soest.hawaii.edu/soestwp/announce/news/scarce-ice-in-moons-darkest-regions/); [LROC](https://lroc.im-ldi.com/images/1255).
- Radar: Clementine bistatic ice claim contradicted; Arecibo finds no massive ice; Mini-RF anomalous craters attributed to boulders; DFSAR 2026 revives a subsurface-ice interpretation for small doubly shadowed craters - [review](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06056v2); [ISRO 2026](https://www.isro.gov.in/ISRO_EN/Chandrayaan2_Dual_Frequency_Synthetic_Aperture_Radar.html).

### Inferences
- For a ranking app, the defensible hierarchy of confidence is: (1) Cabeus (LCROSS ground truth + strongest neutron signal), (2) Shoemaker / Haworth / Faustini (neutron maxima co-located with < 75 K surfaces, many M3 detections, DFSAR candidates), (3) Sverdrup, de Gerlache, Slater, small unnamed PSRs with high M3 detection density, (4) cold but "optically empty" traps (Amundsen, Idel'son L, Wiechert) that are only candidates for buried ice.
- Because surface-frost datasets and the buried-hydrogen dataset do not correlate, a site score should carry two separate sub-scores (surface frost evidence; top-metre hydrogen evidence) rather than one "ice" value.
- ShadowCam (2026) lowers the weight that should be given to M3's high-abundance pixels, but it does not test the buried reservoir that neutron data and LCROSS point to.

### Gaps
- Chandrayaan-2 IIRS: no south-polar ice result located in this search; not covered.
- Spudis et al. 2013 (Mini-RF anomalous crater counts) and Patterson et al. 2017 (bistatic Cabeus) could not be retrieved; the "5-10 wt% in Shackleton from Mini-RF" style numbers are unverified and omitted.
- Paige et al. 2010 per-crater temperature tables were not accessible (abstract only); per-PSR temperatures below come from Lemelin et al. 2021 and McClanahan et al. 2024.
- The ShadowCam 2026 paper itself (PSR names, number of candidate spots) was only available via press releases.
- No peer-reviewed MSolo/TRIDENT science paper from IM-2 found; only NASA slides (April 2026).
- No result of any kind from Chang'e-7 exists (launch slipped to 2027).

## 2. Per-location summary of the major south-polar PSRs and cold traps

### Takeaway
The three largest PSRs (Shoemaker ~1,075 km2, Haworth ~1,018 km2, Faustini ~664 km2) are also the ones where neutron hydrogen maxima coincide with the coldest (59-73 K) surfaces, and Cabeus (PSR ~285-315 km2) holds the only ground truth; among smaller targets, Nobile's small PSRs and Malapert/Mons Mouton PSRs are the most accessible on gentle slopes from sunlit terrain, whereas Shackleton's floor is reachable only via >= 25 deg slopes. Amundsen, Idel'son L and Wiechert are cold traps with no M3 surface-ice detections.

- "Most ice evidence" and "most accessible" are anti-correlated: Shoemaker and Faustini have no route in on <= 10 deg slopes and only 2-5 % illumination within 1 km, whereas Nobile 2, Sverdrup 1 and Malapert are reachable on 0-5 deg access slopes but show weaker hydrogen signals (0.05-0.13 wt% WEH).
- Lemelin's WEH column (e.g. Cabeus 0.90 wt%, PSR 89 0.84 wt%) and McClanahan's LEND collimated WEH (Cabeus 0.39 wt%) are different products; they should not be mixed in one numeric field without tagging the instrument.
- Small unnamed PSRs with high M3 detection densities (PSR 120: 0.41/km2; PSR 57: 0.31/km2; PSR 89: 0.24/km2, 5 deg mean slope) may score better per unit of traverse effort than the giant PSRs.

### Gaps
- No per-crater annual-max/min Diviner values from Paige et al. 2010 itself were retrieved; minimum temperatures in Lemelin's table contain implausible values (2 K, 14 K).
- No quantitative instrument-specific data found for Cabeus B, de Gerlache, Slater, Idel'son L and Wiechert beyond PSR area and qualitative mentions.
- Gazetteer-grade coordinates were directly confirmed only for Cabeus and Amundsen; the others are Wikipedia infobox values (stated to derive from the Gazetteer).
- NASA NTRS 20250008823 (Ahrens et al. 2025, "Diverse Lunar Polar Permanently Shadowed Regions and Environmental Metrics for Site Planning Decision-Making") exists and likely holds per-PSR planning metrics, but its PDF could not be text-extracted: [NTRS PDF](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20250008823/downloads/AhrensDiverseSTI.pdf).

## 3. Total PSR area, size distribution, micro cold traps, seasonal/shallow-subsurface zones, total inventory and its uncertainty, age

### Takeaway
South of 80 deg S the mapped PSR area is ~20,800 km2 (LOLA 20 m/px, 2023), spread over ~466,000 individual PSRs but dominated by a few tens of large ones; cold-trap area (T_max < 110 K) is smaller for mapped scales (~13,000 km2 from Diviner) but ~23,000 km2 in the south when all scales down to 1 cm are modelled (Hayne 2021). Total ice inventory is uncertain by more than an order of magnitude (roughly 1e8 to >1e9 t in published claims), and 2023-2026 results (young PSRs; ShadowCam non-detections) push toward the low end for surface/near-surface ice while 2024 LEND and ISRO analyses argue for widespread but dilute buried hydrogen.

#### 3.2 Shallow-subsurface and seasonally shadowed stability zones in sunlit, landable terrain

- VIPER's four Ice Stability Regions (ISRs), "based on the predicted thermal stability of ice with depth": Surface (ice stable at surface = PSRs), Shallow (stable within 0-50 cm), Deep (stable at 50-100 cm), Dry (0-100 cm too warm) - [VIPER/Colaprete NASEM slides 2021](https://www.nationalacademies.org/cdn/materials/9fba0b0c-198c-4772-a367-136febe32e2a).
- Lemelin 2021 tabulates predicted water-ice stability depth of 0.00-0.11 m (Shoemaker, Cabeus 1) to 0.00-0.43 m (Faustini) inside priority PSRs, and CO2 ice within 0-2.5 m - [Lemelin 2021](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/PSJ/abf3c5).
- Review (2025): vapour-pumped subsurface ice can form where the subsurface peak temperature is ~110 K with no surface frost required; predicted depth is of order the thermal skin depth (~5-10 cm); neutron-inferred burial 3-30 g/cm2; buried relic ice can persist for billions of years at up to ~140 K; "a single borehole at an appropriate site could verify it" - [review arXiv:2502.06056](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06056v2).
- NASA 2022: the sunlit Shackleton connecting ridge "could have ice below the surface" - [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/artemis/clps/nasa-intuitive-machines-announce-landing-site-location-for-lunar-drill/).
- ChaSTE (2025): at 69 deg S, metre-scale slope differences change temperatures drastically; poleward-facing slopes > 14 deg are favourable for shallow subsurface ice stability - [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/science-technology/chandrayaan-3-finds-clues-to-hidden-water-ice-on-the-moon).
- LEND 2024: hydrogen enhancement tracks PSRs out to at least 77 deg S; fewer/smaller/lower-concentration deposits expected in warmer, periodically sunlit areas - [NASA 2024](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/moon/nasas-lro-lunar-ice-deposits-are-widespread/).
- USGS/NASA prospectivity model for the VIPER site (Coyan et al. 2025, PSJ, DOI 10.3847/PSJ/adbc6c) uses topography, slope, aspect, curvature, maximum temperature, depth to ice stability, PSRs, distance to PSRs and PSR density to predict ice likelihood in the top metre; classes: highly prospective / favourable / permissive / non-prospective - [astrobiology.com summary](https://astrobiology.com/2025/05/07/astrochemistry-prospectivity-modeling-of-the-nasa-viper-landing-site-at-mons-mouton-near-the-lunar-south-pole/); [USGS record](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70266320).

#### 3.4 Age and results that narrow or widen ice extent (2016-2026)

- Schorghofer & Rufu 2023 (Science Advances, DOI 10.1126/sciadv.adh4302): most PSRs arose < 2.2 Ga ago; PSR area 2.2 Ga ago about half of today's, ~3.1 Ga ago about a quarter, negligible before ~3.4 Ga; solar declination was 3 deg (twice today's 1.5 deg) ~2.1 Ga ago and 2 deg 0.61 Ga ago; average PSR age <= ~1.8 Ga; Cassini-state transition ~4.1 Ga; "overall estimates for the amount of cold-trapped ices have to be revised downward"; volcanic outgassing peak (3.7-3.5 Ga) predates most PSRs; the 0.9 Ga LCROSS PSR implies volatiles were still delivered and trapped in the Copernican - [Schorghofer & Rufu 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10499310/).
- Siegler et al. 2016 (Nature 531:480): polar hydrogen pattern is antipodal and offset from both present poles, interpreted as true polar wander (palaeopole) driven by the Procellarum thermal anomaly - [Caltech record](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/mw7br-nw117).
- Li et al. 2018: patchiness attributed to low supply rate, fast gardening, possible polar wander; surface frost may persist no longer than ~20 Myr against gardening - [Li 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/6130389).
- Widening: Hayne 2021 micro cold traps (2020/21); LEND 2024 ("widespread" to 77 deg S); ISRO 2024; DFSAR 2026 - sources above.
- Narrowing: Schorghofer & Rufu 2023; ShadowCam/Li 2026 (surface ice scarce; Moon lacks the near-pure deposits of Mercury/Ceres although its poles are colder; proposed causes: space weathering by solar wind, volcanic degassing history, impact mixing) - [SOEST 2026](https://www.soest.hawaii.edu/soestwp/announce/news/scarce-ice-in-moons-darkest-regions/).
- Loss/supply numbers from the 2025 review: sublimation ~100 kg m^-2 Ga^-1 at 109 K and ~1,000 kg m^-2 Ga^-1 at 114 K (factor ~10 per 5 K); pessimistic space-weathering loss ~0.2 nm/yr; comets now considered a negligible source; vapour pumping ~one monolayer per year (~30 cm/Ga) would suffice - [review](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06056v2).

### Inferences
- Illustrative arithmetic (not from a source): 20,800 km2 x NASA's ">= 5 L per m2" gives ~1e11 L, i.e. order 100 million t as a neutron-based lower bound for PSRs south of 80 deg S if the LEND interpretation holds; the LCROSS grade applied to the same area would give more than an order of magnitude more. The spread between these is the real uncertainty.
- Because PSR age and ice evidence do not correlate (Cabeus young but richest; Shackleton old but poor), age should not be used as a positive predictor in site scoring; temperature at the base of poleward-facing slopes (< 75 K) is the better-supported predictor.
- "Shallow" and "deep" ISR terrain is, by definition, outside permanent shadow; maps of depth-to-ice-stability are therefore the key layer for finding ice prospects in landable, at least intermittently sunlit terrain.

### Gaps
- Mazarico et al. 2011 totals (commonly quoted as 16,055 km2 south / 12,866 km2 north poleward of 80 deg) could not be confirmed from the NTRS/PGDA pages; treat as unverified. PGDA product page (maps at 240/120/60 m/px, PSR rasters): [PGDA product 69](https://pgda.gsfc.nasa.gov/products/69).
- No km2 figure was found for subsurface-only (shallow/deep) stability area outside PSRs for the whole south polar cap; Schorghofer & Williams 2020 full text not accessed.
- No formally published uncertainty range for the total inventory; all ranges above come from disparate methods.

## 4. Physical form of the ice, implications for extraction, trafficability and operating at ~40 K

### Takeaway
The working picture is dilute ice (order 0.1-5 wt%) mixed with or buried in porous regolith beneath a centimetres-to-decimetres dry layer, plus rare thin surface frost; nothing indicates thick, clean, Mercury-like slabs. NASA's ISRU threshold for a "reserve" is >= 2 wt% within 5-100 cm depth over a 500 m radius, and its own case study says 1 wt% is unlikely to be viable; PSR regolith appears trafficable (bearing capacity similar to or stronger than elsewhere) but access slopes, 40-100 K temperatures, darkness and loss of Earth line-of-sight dominate the engineering risk.

### Inferences
- With NASA's 2 wt% reserve threshold, only Cabeus has measured evidence above threshold (LCROSS); every neutron-derived value (0.2-0.9 wt%) is below it unless the ice is concentrated in patches or under a dry lid - which is exactly what VIPER-class drilling must resolve.
- NASA's own comparison (4.9 t / 68 kW for water vs 2.7 t / 45 kW for oxygen-from-regolith, plus "delay water mining demonstration until better knowledge is obtained") means a base-site score should not hinge solely on ice; proximity to ice is an option value, not a proven supply.
- The < 5 km and < 20 deg ISRU criteria can be applied directly as hard filters between a sunlit base site and its nearest candidate PSR.

### Gaps
- No in-situ geotechnical measurement of PSR regolith exists; boulder-track work covered 70-76 deg latitude, not the deep polar PSRs.
- No sources found on battery chemistry, power beaming or electrostatic dust charging specifically at 40 K.
- Depth profile of ice (frost vs pore-filling vs layers) is unmeasured anywhere at the south pole.

## 5. Other resources relevant to a base (oxygen, metals, solar-wind volatiles, Th/KREEP, construction material) and how they vary by site

### Takeaway
Non-ice resources at the south pole are essentially uniform highland regolith: > 40 % oxygen by mass, > 75 % anorthite (Al, Ca, Si source), iron- and ilmenite-poor (< 2 vol% ilmenite), so whole-regolith oxygen processes (carbothermal, molten regolith electrolysis) are favoured over ilmenite reduction; these resources are "accessible and ubiquitous" and barely discriminate between south-polar sites. Site-to-site differences come mainly from ice proximity, illumination and a modest mafic (SPA-related) component, not from ore grade.

### Inferences
- For ranking south-polar sites, regolith oxygen/metals/construction feedstock can be treated as a constant; the variables that actually differ by site are (a) distance and slope to PSRs with hydrogen/ice evidence, (b) presence of extra-cold (< 60 K) traps holding CO2/N/S/C-bearing volatiles (useful for carbon and nitrogen supply), and (c) illumination/communications.
- Thorium/KREEP and He-3 are not credible differentiators inside the polar cap on present data.

### Gaps
- No in-situ compositional measurement exists poleward of ~70 deg S; site-specific Th (ppm), TiO2 and FeO values for individual polar craters were not found.
- No measured He-3 or solar-wind-H concentration exists for PSR regolith ("no data exists" per a 2014 synthesis: [AAPG abstract](https://www.searchanddiscovery.com/abstracts/html/2014/90189ace/abstracts/1839528.html)).
- Sintering/3D-printing performance of polar highland regolith was not researched here.

## 6. NASA's stated resource strategy, favourability maps, VIPER

### Takeaway
NASA's strategy is a dual ISRU path (polar water mining and oxygen/metals from regolith) focused initially on the south pole, with water mining demonstrations explicitly deferred until ground truth exists; its measurement plan calls for a predictive "water favorability" model, more orbital analysis and three tiers of landed prospecting before a pilot plant. As of November 2026 the ground-truth step is still ahead: VIPER is re-manifested on Blue Origin's second Blue Moon MK1 for late 2027 at Mons Mouton, and NASA's 2026 "Moon Base" plan starts with robotic south-pole missions (first landing target: Shackleton connecting ridge).

### Inferences
- NASA's published criteria can be turned into scoring rules directly: reserve >= 2 wt% within 1 m; ISR "shallow/deep" terrain near the base; < 5 km and < 20 deg from base to resource; > 220 days sunlight; HLS access 84-90 deg S; lander tilt <= 8 deg; slope < 10 deg plus > 45 % sunlight/Earth visibility (LROC criterion).
- The official ordering of activities (regolith oxygen demonstrations first, water mining deferred; first Moon Base landing at the Shackleton connecting ridge; VIPER at Mons Mouton in 2027) implies that near-term base siting is driven by illumination/communications/landing safety, with ice as a secondary, yet-to-be-proven criterion.
- The Mons Mouton / Nobile sector is the only place where NASA will, on current plans, have ground truth across all four ISR classes before ~2028, which raises its information value relative to better-endowed but unexplored PSRs (Shoemaker, Faustini, Cabeus).

### Gaps
- VIPER's updated 2027 landing coordinates/traverse for the Blue Moon MK1 delivery were not found; the 2021 values may be superseded.
- Whether the first Blue Moon MK1 ("Endurance") has launched/landed by November 2026 was not confirmed (NASA pages give "NET fall 2026" and also "2027"); a claim that New Glenn suffered a pad explosion in May 2026 comes from a low-reliability aggregator and is unverified: [spaceodysseyhub](https://spaceodysseyhub.com/moon/landers/blue-moon-mk1-viper).
- Reports that NASA's Moon Base is "locked" to the Shackleton rim/connecting ridge with a $20-30 billion budget come from secondary outlets ([TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/317212/20260526/nasa-moon-base-30b-79-launch-plan-locks-shackleton-crater-2036.htm), [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/nasa-unveils-new-ambitious-moon-base-plans/)); NASA's own pages read here name the Shackleton Connecting Ridge only as the Endurance landing site and give no budget.
- Full text of Brown et al. 2022 (per-PSR scores, tonnages) and Cannon & Britt 2020 (IFI inputs) is paywalled/blocked; only abstracts and institutional summaries were used.
- The Lunar Site Selection white paper PDF could only be partially text-extracted; no explicit volatile criteria could be quoted from it.
