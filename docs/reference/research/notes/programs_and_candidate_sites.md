# NASA lunar program and lunar-base plans: south-pole landing/base site selection (state as of 14 Nov 2026)

Conventions used in these notes
- Coordinates are decimal degrees, latitude negative = south, longitude east-positive. Where a source uses 0-360 E, the -180..180 value is given too.
- "FLOWN" = happened; "PLANNED" = not yet flown as of 14 Nov 2026; "SUPERSEDED" = an older plan that has since been changed.
- Source dates are given in brackets. Wikipedia and tracker sites are secondary and are marked as such.
- 1 degree of latitude on the Moon is about 30.3 km (R = 1737.4 km); used only in the Inferences sections.

## 1. Status and schedule of Artemis II, III, IV, V and the Human Landing System (as of Nov 2026)

### Takeaway
Artemis II flew (1-10 Apr 2026, crewed lunar flyby). On 27 Feb 2026 NASA re-planned the campaign: Artemis III is no longer a landing but a crewed low-Earth-orbit docking test with both landers (2027), and the first crewed south-pole landing moves to Artemis IV (2028) with Artemis V as a second 2028 landing; no lander is assigned to a specific landing mission, Gateway is paused, SLS Block 1B is dropped, and the lunar rendezvous orbit is no longer NRHO.

### Inferences
- For a landing-window model, the Oct 2026 change of rendezvous orbit matters: all published Artemis III site/window analyses (2022-2026 NTRS abstract) assumed NRHO and a 5.75-6.5 day stay; the 2028 missions now assume low lunar orbit (Starship) or a 100 x 6,500 km polar ellipse (Blue Moon) and short sortie EVAs, so NRHO-derived launch-window constraints are likely outdated.
- The earliest crewed south-pole landing is 2028, and it could slip: Artemis III itself is reported 90 days behind, both landers need an uncrewed lunar demo first, and New Glenn had not returned to flight by early Nov 2026.

### Gaps
- No NASA statement dated November 2026 with a specific Artemis III or IV launch date was found; "mid-2027" vs "late 2027" for Artemis III remains unresolved.
- The OIG full report (IG-26-004) text on slope tolerance, lander height and tip-over margins was not retrieved; only the press summary and news coverage were read.
- No GAO report from 2026 on Artemis schedule was retrieved.
- Artemis IV/V crew, surface-stay length in days and exact SLS upper stage are not confirmed by a primary source.

## 2. Artemis III candidate landing regions (13 of Aug 2022, 9 of Oct 2024), coordinates, sizes, criteria, later down-selection

### Takeaway
NASA has published names but no official centre coordinates for its regions; the best citable coordinates are the LROC NAC mosaic centres and bounds built for each "Artemis III candidate landing site", listed below. No further down-selection after October 2024 was found: as of Oct-Nov 2026 the landing site for the first crewed landing (now Artemis IV) is undecided and NASA says more robotic-lander data is needed.

### Cited Findings

#### 2a. Original 13 regions (NASA release 22-089, 19 Aug 2022) — now SUPERSEDED for the first landing

- The 13: Faustini Rim A, Peak Near Shackleton, Connecting Ridge, Connecting Ridge Extension, de Gerlache Rim 1, de Gerlache Rim 2, de Gerlache-Kocher Massif, Haworth, Malapert Massif, Leibnitz Beta Plateau, Nobile Rim 1, Nobile Rim 2, Amundsen Rim. — [NASA release, 19 Aug 2022](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-identifies-candidate-regions-for-landing-next-americans-on-moon/)
- Each region is about 15 x 15 km (9.3 x 9.3 miles); a landing site is a location within a region with a radius of about 100 m (328 ft); all regions lie within 6 degrees of latitude of the South Pole. — [NASA release, 19 Aug 2022](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-identifies-candidate-regions-for-landing-next-americans-on-moon/)
- 2022 criteria: terrain slope, ease of communications with Earth, lighting conditions; launch-window availability (several regions so that launches are possible throughout the year); combined capabilities of SLS, Orion and Starship HLS; proximity to permanently shadowed regions for a moonwalk; all 13 regions contain sites with continuous sunlight throughout a 6.5-day surface stay. — [NASA release, 19 Aug 2022](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-identifies-candidate-regions-for-landing-next-americans-on-moon/)
- Specific sites within regions are chosen only after target launch dates are set, because the dates fix transfer trajectories and surface conditions. — [NASA release, 19 Aug 2022](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-identifies-candidate-regions-for-landing-next-americans-on-moon/)
- Data: LRO data, LOLA DEM and SPICE ephemerides (SVS visualization 5013, released 19 Aug 2022). — [NASA SVS 5013](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5013)

#### 2b. Refined 9 regions (NASA release 24-136, 28 Oct 2024; announced at LEAG, JSC)

- The 9, in no priority order: Peak near Cabeus B, Haworth, Malapert Massif, Mons Mouton Plateau, Mons Mouton, Nobile Rim 1, Nobile Rim 2, de Gerlache Rim 2, Slater Plain. — [NASA release, 28 Oct 2024](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-provides-update-on-artemis-iii-moon-landing-regions/)
- 2024 criteria: science potential, launch window availability, terrain suitability, communication capabilities with Earth, lighting conditions, and the combined trajectory capabilities of SLS, Orion and Starship HLS; analysis by the Cross Agency Site Selection Analysis team using LRO data; no sizes or coordinates given in the release. — [NASA release, 28 Oct 2024](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-provides-update-on-artemis-iii-moon-landing-regions/)
- Next steps stated in 2024: geologic maps, community workshops, continued survey of the whole South Pole region, expanded science for Artemis IV, and LTV suitability for Artemis V. — [NASA release, 28 Oct 2024](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-provides-update-on-artemis-iii-moon-landing-regions/)
- Dropped from the 13: Faustini Rim A, Peak Near Shackleton, Connecting Ridge, Connecting Ridge Extension, de Gerlache Rim 1, de Gerlache-Kocher Massif, Leibnitz Beta Plateau (renamed area, see Mons Mouton), Amundsen Rim. New in the 9: Peak near Cabeus B, Mons Mouton Plateau, Mons Mouton, Slater Plain. (Derived by comparing the two lists.) — [NASA release, 19 Aug 2022](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-identifies-candidate-regions-for-landing-next-americans-on-moon/); [NASA release, 28 Oct 2024](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-provides-update-on-artemis-iii-moon-landing-regions/)

#### 2c. NASA's own description of the 13-to-9 down-selection (George, Rubio, Lawrence, Curell; LPSC 2026 abstract, NTRS 20250011660)

All points below are from — [NTRS 20250011660, LPSC 2026 abstract](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20250011660) ([docx](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20250011660/downloads/AIIIUpdateLPSC2026.docx)):
- Assumptions evolved with SpaceX HLS vehicle performance and trajectory analyses; assessment emphasised end-to-end mission availability: launch opportunities, transit and rendezvous geometry, communication constraints, landing performance, surface illumination and terrain safety, evaluated simultaneously on a calendar basis.
- In-space operations assumed NRHO (SUPERSEDED by the Oct 2026 architecture); NRHO "imposes strong temporal and geometric constraints on surface access".
- Surface stay varies from about 5.75 to 6.25 days depending on mission epoch plus low-lunar-orbit rev considerations.
- Terrain: minimise slope variability within a landing ellipse; prioritise regions with enough low-slope area; evaluate craters, block populations and rough terrain; favour regions where hazards can be mitigated by site placement.
- Communications: if Direct-to-Earth is used, regions with greater Earth visibility are preferred.
- Illumination: required for the entire crewed surface stay; south-polar illumination has strong seasonal variability and shifts about 2-3 weeks per year; regions robust across mission windows are favoured.
- Individual regions "typically provide limited viable opportunities when all constraints are simultaneously satisfied"; more than one region must be kept to give acceptable annual mission availability.
- Removed regions are not unsuitable for future exploration; the nine are specific to Artemis III's crew-safety emphasis; the refinement "does not affect site selection for Artemis IV and later missions", which are expected to reach broader parts of the south polar region.

#### 2e. Numeric criteria used in published site studies (useful thresholds for a ranking app)

- NASA-defined "accessible" terrain for Artemis III: slopes below 5 degrees, sunlit, and with direct-to-Earth visibility at the time of the surface mission; two astronauts on foot within 2 km of the HLS; 6 Earth-day surface mission. — [LPSC 2024 no. 1695](https://www.hou.usra.edu/meetings/lpsc2024/pdf/1695.pdf)
- Gracy and Lee screening: 2 km-radius exploration zone (12.57 km2) around a 100 m x 100 m landing spot; slope at or below 5 degrees over at least 25% of the zone; solar illumination at least 33% of the time; DTE visibility at least 50% of the time. — [LPSC 2024 no. 1695](https://www.hou.usra.edu/meetings/lpsc2024/pdf/1695.pdf)
- South Pole-Aitken material fraction: Connecting Ridge 46% (rank 3 of 13), Peak Near Shackleton 47% (rank 2 of 13). — [LPSC 2024 no. 1695](https://www.hou.usra.edu/meetings/lpsc2024/pdf/1695.pdf)
- TOPSIS study: slope 0-5 degrees with 100 m buffer gave 732 polygons (25 m2 to 25,000 m2) and 1,247 sample points at 50 m spacing; inputs were LOLA DEM/slope at 5 m/px (about 90% interpolated), illumination/PSR/Earth-shine maps at 240 m/px, M3 at 1.4 km/px; criteria: visible area from 40 m height, lander visibility within 2 km walking radius, illumination x PSR share within 2 km, Earth communication proxy, geologic units, mafic abundance. Points per region: Nobile Rim 2 473, Amundsen Rim 149, de Gerlache Rim 2 104, Malapert Massif 102, Nobile Rim 1 94, Faustini Rim A 80, de Gerlache-Kocher 56, Site04 56, Connecting Ridge Extension 36, Leibnitz Beta Plateau 23, Haworth 20, Connecting Ridge 20, Peak Near Shackleton 19, de Gerlache Rim 1 15. — [arXiv 2406.19863v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19863v2)
- Wueller et al. 2026 (JGR Planets 131, e2025JE009434): 130 candidate landing sites inside Artemis candidate regions that meet HLS requirements and reach PSRs within 2 km (the walking limit without a rover); Table A1 lists all 130 with coordinates. — [JGR Planets, DOI 10.1029/2025JE009434](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2025JE009434)
- Connecting Ridge (Shackleton-de Gerlache) illumination: 92.27-95.65% of the time at 2-10 m above ground; longest continuous darkness 3-5 days. — [Wikipedia Lunar south pole, secondary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_south_pole)

Treat the centres as approximate region positions (probably good to a few km), not as official NASA region centres.
- The 2024 "Mons Mouton" region is probably covered by the LROC "Nobile Rim 1 and Mons Mouton (Leibnitz Beta Plateau)" mosaic, and "de Gerlache Rim 2" by the single "de Gerlache Rim" mosaic; the mapping of names to mosaics is my reading of the product titles, not a NASA statement.
- The 2024 list moved away from the regions closest to the pole (Connecting Ridge, Peak Near Shackleton, de Gerlache Rim 1, Faustini Rim A all dropped) toward lower-latitude, more Earth-visible terrain (Mons Mouton, Malapert, Nobile, Cabeus B). ## 3. Artemis Base Camp concept, its 2026 successor "Moon Base", and architecture documents defining site requirements

### Takeaway
"Artemis Base Camp" has been superseded in 2026 by NASA's "Moon Base" programme (announced 24 Mar 2026, three phases to 2032 and beyond) located at the lunar South Pole; the only named site so far is the Shackleton Connecting Ridge, where the first Moon Base mission (Blue Moon MK1 "Endurance") is to land, and NASA says the base location will only be confirmed from Phase 1 robotic scouting.

#### 4c. Lessons for landing near the pole

- IM-1: the laser rangefinders could not be used because a physical safety switch was not enabled before launch; a NASA Navigation Doppler Lidar payload was patched in as the landing sensor hours before landing; the lander came in fast, a leg caught and it tipped; precision goal of 100 m was missed (about 1.5 km). — [Payload, Feb 2024](https://payloadspace.com/im-1-is-sideways-but-weve-all-been-there/); [Moon Monday no. 165](https://jatan.space/moon-monday-issue-165/)
- IM-2: the rangefinder returned optical noise even when the laser was not firing ("the measurements were all over the place"); three orbits of troubleshooting failed; waiting longer would have moved the site outside the onboard crater maps; touchdown at 10-13 m/s while software believed it was still about 400 m up; landed near a crater edge, ended on its side in shadow so solar arrays could not recharge. — [Aerospace America, 22 Jul 2025](https://aerospaceamerica.aiaa.org/how-intuitive-machines-is-planning-to-make-its-third-moon-landing-a-successful-one/); [Spaceflight Now, 7 Mar 2025](https://spaceflightnow.com/2025/03/07/intuitive-machines-im-2-mission-ends-with-lander-on-its-side-on-the-moon/)
- IM-3 changes: primary and redundant rangefinders from two vendors covering 15 km to 5 km and terminal descent; airplane and helicopter sensor tests; larger crater-map library; 12 lunar orbits before landing; review of ground-contact conditions, stability and landing-gear durability. — [Aerospace America, 22 Jul 2025](https://aerospaceamerica.aiaa.org/how-intuitive-machines-is-planning-to-make-its-third-moon-landing-a-successful-one/)
- Chandrayaan-3: landing zone about 16 km2 selected from Chandrayaan-2 orbiter imagery; descent hovers about 30 s at about 150 m to pick a clear spot; fixes versus Chandrayaan-2 were more attitude authority (25 deg/s vs 10 deg/s), laser Doppler velocimetry, stronger legs, more redundancy; surface peaked at 355 K on a 6-degree sun-facing slope vs about 332 K on flat ground 1 m away; exhaust removed about 3 cm of regolith. — [Wikipedia Chandrayaan-3, secondary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandrayaan-3)
- Luna-25: lost during a pre-landing orbit burn, i.e. before any polar-terrain effect. — [SpacePolicyOnline, 2023](https://spacepolicyonline.com/news/nasas-lro-spots-likely-luna-25-crash-site)
- Chang'e-7: the launch date is constrained by the need to land in the best lighting at Shackleton, so missing the August 2026 window pushed the mission to 2027. — [Space.com, Aug 2026](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/bad-weather-delays-launch-of-chinas-historic-change-7-mission-to-moons-south-pole); [SpaceNews, Aug 2026](https://spacenews.com/china-delays-change-7-lunar-south-pole-landing-mission-launch/)
- OIG and ASAP on crewed landers: Starship's height creates tip-over risk; cryogenic propellants have never been used on the Moon; only one uncrewed landing test per provider is planned. — [The Register, 11 Mar 2026](https://www.theregister.com/2026/03/11/nasa_oig_artemis_lander/); [SpacePolicyOnline, Oct 2026](https://spacepolicyonline.com/news/nasa-is-full-steam-ahead-on-artemis-iii-iv-and-v/)

### Inferences
- Both US polar landers tipped over, and in both cases the proximate cause was altimetry, not slope or lighting directly. The polar setting made the consequences worse: long shadows, crater-rich terrain and low Sun meant a lander on its side in a small crater lost power quickly.
- Lighting-constrained landing windows at near-pole sites are narrow enough that a launch slip of weeks can cost a year (Chang'e-7), which supports modelling windows per site and per season rather than as a single annual figure.
- No mission has yet tested a landing poleward of 85 S; the first attempts (Blue Moon MK1 at the Connecting Ridge, Chang'e-7 at Shackleton) are now both 2027 events, so NASA's wish for more robotic data before choosing the crewed site may collide with the 2028 landing goal.

### Gaps
- IM-1 coordinates (80.13 S, 1.44 E) and Chang'e-7 target coordinates come from secondary sources or search-result excerpts; no LROC or CNSA primary page was read for them.
- Chang'e-6 and SLIM landing coordinates and lessons were not verified in this session (both are non-polar: Chang'e-6 on the far side in the SPA basin, SLIM near the equator).
- No landing coordinates have been published for Blue Moon MK1 "Endurance", Griffin-1, VIPER, IM-4, IM-5, PROMISE or Chang'e-8.
- No official return-to-flight date for New Glenn or November 2026 status for Endurance was found; Q1 2027 comes from launch-listing sites and Wikipedia.
- ShadowCam findings specific to individual Artemis regions were not retrieved.

## 5. What NASA documents say about sites for a long-term base versus a short first sortie

### Takeaway
NASA treats the two as different problems: the first sortie prioritises crew safety and mission availability (low slopes, continuous sunlight for the stay, direct-to-Earth visibility, several regions to keep launch windows open), while base siting is deferred to Phase 1 robotic scouting and is tied to sustained power, ice access, mobility and communications; NASA has not published a ranking of sites for either purpose.

### Inferences
- A ranking app should probably score sortie sites and base sites with different weights. Sortie: low slope within a 100 m landing spot and a 2 km walking zone, continuous sunlight for a stay of days, DTE visibility, and number of usable windows per year. Base: annual illumination fraction and longest darkness, distance to PSRs within rover range, room for separated landing pads, and line-of-sight for towers.
- By NASA's own choices, lower-latitude plateau sites (Mons Mouton, Malapert, Nobile) fit the sortie profile and the Shackleton-de Gerlache Connecting Ridge fits the base profile; this is an inference from the 2024 list and the Moon Base I target, not a NASA ranking.
- With the Oct 2026 sortie reduced to two 2-hour EVAs, the 6-day continuous-illumination requirement that shaped the nine regions is probably looser for the first landing than it was in 2024.

### Gaps
- No NASA document was found that ranks named sites for a base or states quantitative base-site thresholds (illumination fraction, maximum slope, pad separation, distance to PSR).
- The Moon Base User's Guide and NTRS 20260008640, which likely hold such requirements, were not readable in this session.
- NASA has not said whether the first crewed landing must be near the eventual base site.
