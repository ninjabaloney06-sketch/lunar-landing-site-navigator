## Mandatory site assessments — confirmed by ninja, 14 Nov 2026

These are **must-do requirements, not bonuses**. This scope decision supersedes earlier optional/excluded treatment in the build plan and briefs; implementation and S's scientific validation remain open.

- **Terrain slope:** assess landing-site terrain slope and exploration access terrain separately. Landing requires **slope ≤ 5°**: above 5° is unsuitable for landing; exactly 5° passes this slope criterion. Passing the slope criterion alone does not establish overall landing safety. Missing slope data leaves landing suitability unknown. Exploration requires **slope < 20°**; exactly 20° or above does not meet this requirement. Both thresholds are ninja's project criteria, not sourced NASA or vehicle safety limits. State the DEM resolution and measurement baseline; one site's slope alone does not establish route accessibility.
- **Surface roughness and surrounding obstacles:** assess roughness and nearby hazards with sourced data, a stated assessment area, scale/resolution and detection limits. Missing or unresolved obstacles are unknown, not evidence of clear terrain.
- **Thermal stability:** assess site-specific temperature variation and extremes over the relevant period using sourced observations or a documented model. Illumination alone is not a validated temperature or thermal-stability result; S must define and validate the interpretation.
- **Scientific value, PSRs and accessibility:** explain site-specific scientific opportunities, identify relevant permanently shadowed regions (PSRs), and assess access from the site using terrain, slope and obstacles. Proximity alone does not establish reachability; PSR presence does not prove usable ice. Use the evidence rules in [lunar-interest-points.md](../reference/lunar-interest-points.md).

For each assessment, show evidence/provenance and limitations, with explicit unknowns when data is missing. An unknown is honest reporting, not completion of the assessment. S reviews the methods and results; Q checks calculations and boundary cases; D integrates the results. Track completion in [TASKS.md](../../TASKS.md#mandatory-site-assessments--confirmed-14-nov-2026). Full automated route optimisation is not implied by the accessibility requirement.

## User and answer

The primary user is a **mission planner** deciding which lunar site and mission period offer the best average communications coverage while providing enough sunlight and useful conditions for future operations.

**Confirmed demo question:** At Connecting Ridge from 16–29 August 2027 UTC, when is the site sunlit, and when can at least one selected Earth station communicate with it?

**Decided (ninja, 14 Nov ~13:15): the demo site is Connecting Ridge, not the Shackleton Rim Site04 point.** Connecting Ridge (−89.46°, −136.68°) is already in the site list with its source. Reason: the Data Book's Site04 coordinate is the centre of a DEM polygon, not a point on the rim (Data Book section A2.1, p. 57); in the LOLA DEM that centre lies on Shackleton's inner wall with a 31.8° slope, fails the 5° landing-slope rule and comes out dark for the whole period. Evidence and the unvalidated preview numbers: [validation.md](validation.md#shackleton-rim-site04-coordinate--source-check-integrator-ai-14-nov-1308-not-validated). Site04 stays in the list, labelled as a region centre. Wherever this document says "Shackleton" for the demo question, the two 2027 periods or the basic comparison, read "Connecting Ridge"; the period ids `shackleton-2027-08` and `shackleton-2027-09` keep their names.

**Answer sentence template:** “At [site] during [period], sunlight is available for [hours / percentage], and at least one selected station has [modeled usable link / geometric visibility only] for [hours / percentage]; [longest blackout] is the longest communications gap.” Never fill this with unvalidated numbers or describe geometry alone as a working link.

## Decisions and scope

- **Coverage:** the user selects Earth ground stations; the objective is the highest average coverage over the mission, measured as the fraction of its elapsed time with at least one selected station available. Do not add overlapping station windows twice. Recalculate with other stations when a station cannot support the link. Identify the station(s) and reason for a gap. A real usable link depends on equipment, availability and propagation as well as line of sight; missing inputs must yield **unknown**, not an invented working link or fabricated outage. **Decided (ninja, 14 Nov): first build uses geometric line of sight at both ends** (station above the lunar terrain mask and Moon above the station's elevation mask); outage inputs stay `unknown` and the contract keeps room for them. **Station set (ninja, 14 Nov): NASA DSN ([Goldstone, Madrid, Canberra](https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/somd/space-communications-navigation-program/dsn-complexes/)) plus ESA ESTRACK deep-space antennas (Cebreros, Malargüe, New Norcia).** Each antenna's coordinates, reference frame and elevation mask need a source link before use. **As built (14 Nov): `stations.csv` holds 5 antennas, DSN (DSS-24, DSS-54, DSS-34) and JAXA (Usuda 64 m, Uchinoura 34 m); ESTRACK is not included because no sourced elevation mask was found (BUILD_LOG 14 Nov). Say "DSN + JAXA", never "DSN + ESTRACK", until ESTRACK rows exist.**
- **Observer and geometry:** 1.75 m above local terrain; planetocentric MOON_ME, east-positive longitude; LT+S, subject to Leo's comparison against Horizons; 300 km maximum terrain-search distance, not a fixed distance to the visible horizon. Terrain and lunar curvature determine the horizon by direction; mark truncated rays and quantify limitations. A smooth 1.75 m observer's lunar horizon is approximately 2.47 km, while distant elevated terrain may still obstruct the sky. Crater ejecta rays are unrelated to horizon-search rays. See [illumination-model study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6398960/).
- **Dates:** supported coverage 2026-11-01 00:00 UTC inclusive to 2031-01-01 00:00 UTC exclusive; 30-minute ephemeris steps. Date-only ranges below include the final named day and end at 00:00 UTC the next day. Interpolate and validate window edges; do not imply 30-minute sampling alone proves edge precision.
- **Finder:** proposed defaults retained from the plan: 14 days, at least 70% sunlit, at least 50% combined station coverage, no coverage gap over 48 h, no darkness over 72 h; all adjustable. Rank qualifying windows by combined coverage, with ties and missing inputs explicitly shown. **Decided (ninja, 14 Nov): build with these as adjustable defaults; Leo reviews the values and rationale in parallel.** Finder before time scrubber; simple 3D-first UI with Sun, Earth, Moon and stars. Preserve a usable non-WebGL answer.
- **Units:** degrees for angles, kilometres for distances unless `_m`, hours/days for duration, percentages for coverage; ISO 8601 UTC timestamps. Site-scene local ENU coordinates may use metres and must be identified as such. These conventions are explicit, not a claim that every NASA dataset uses them.
- **Site information:** offer the [nine 2024 NASA candidate regions](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-provides-update-on-artemis-iii-moon-landing-regions/): Peak near Cabeus B, Haworth, Malapert Massif, Mons Mouton Plateau, Mons Mouton, Nobile Rim 1, Nobile Rim 2, de Gerlache Rim 2 and Slater Plain. Label historical regions separately. Connecting Ridge is a separate Blue Moon MK1 target, not a 2024 Artemis region. Regions are areas: identify the exact representative point, source, coordinate frame and uncertainty; do not claim a representative point is NASA's chosen final landing site.
- **Site benefits:** each site's entry must explain its sourced potential for a lunar base and future missions, including site-specific evidence or explicit unknowns for sunlight, terrain/access, communications, science and proximity to permanently shadowed regions or potential water ice. Do not infer usable ice from shadow alone or repeat generic claims as site-specific evidence. The [NASA south-pole overview](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/moonbase-environment/) and [site study](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2025JE009434) provide starting points; individual site claims need checking.
- **Nearby interests research:** [Lunar points of interest](../reference/lunar-interest-points.md) defines the sourced catalogue, proposed infographic, evidence labels and proximity/access rules (14 Nov). Research/specification only; no implementation or scientific validation claimed.
- **Energy and trajectory:** proposed decision, delegated by ninja: keep the ideal-energy and burn assessment outside the event submission, as in the later TASKS item and build plan. No SLS or efficiency data in the event core.

## Comparison points and periods

**Proposed reference points, not proven optimal:** NASA's [Lunar Surface Data Book, table A2-2](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20230007818/downloads/ACD-50044%20Lunar%20Surface%20Data%20Book%20Rev%20A.pdf) lists Shackleton Rim Site04 at latitude −89.7668°, longitude −171.87°, and Malapert Massif Site23 at −85.9948°, −0.23578°. They are reproducible reference locations; verify the source frame and coordinate conversion before using them. A numerical optimum cannot be chosen without the agreed objective, terrain, station and link inputs, and Leo's validation.

Compare Connecting Ridge (formerly Shackleton Rim, see the 13:15 decision above) for **16–29 August 2027** and **12–26 September 2027**, and Malapert for **21–27 December 2026** and **9–15 January 2028**. These are team-supplied candidate periods, **not verified best windows**. **Decided (ninja, 14 Nov): they are the named test and demo periods; the finder computes the actual best windows, and any difference is shown, not hidden.** Report calculations, limitations and alternatives before ranking them. ## Acceptance criteria

- **UX-01 — correct observer and provenance:** Sun and station calculations identify the lunar site, coordinates, frame, UTC and units. Reject wrong-body observers, Earth-Moon barycentre as Earth, and RA/Dec-only references for azimuth/elevation validation. Q checks the reference configuration; Leo signs Sun/Earth and station geometry against independent references. Results state their source and limitations.
- **UX-02 — direct controls:** A signed-out, unbriefed person can choose a site, date range and stations without setup dialogs, and see sunlight and station coverage together. On desktop and a 375 px touch viewport, answer the confirmed demo question within 60 seconds of opening a fresh URL; no output-column setup.
- **UX-03 — robust core:** Show loading, empty and error states. Invalid or out-of-coverage inputs cannot display NaN or stale values under a new selection. If the 3D/map view fails, the site list, numbers and timeline remain usable. Unknown station availability or link conditions are visibly unknown, not blue active links.
- **UX-04 — reachable sites and numbers:** Every offered site is selectable from a list; any map uses a suitable polar view. Mouse and touch reveal Sun and Earth/station azimuth and elevation as applicable, numerical values and windows. Compare values with Leo's independently validated results.
- **UX-05 — one answer:** One flow presents sunlight, combined selected-station coverage, overlap, daily windows, summaries and selected site/date/station context. Yellow indicates sunlight, black/dark indicates none, and blue indicates a modeled active communications link; provide text/pattern labels and a distinct unknown state. The reference image is a visual mockup with fictional terrain and values, not scientific evidence.
- **Basic comparison:** With two Leo-validated sites and two supported periods, switch Connecting Ridge ↔ Malapert over the same period and switch the two specified Connecting Ridge periods at one site. Context and results update together; after load each change produces a readable answer within 10 seconds. Optional side-by-side layout, map and score may be cut; basic comparison may not. Record timings, failures, fixes and Leo's sign-off in `validation.md`; none is yet passed.

## Judging map

- **Impact:** site and period comparison helps a planner assess sunlight and communications coverage.
- **Creativity:** terrain, selected Earth stations and the combined timeline connect geometry to a concrete planning choice.
- **Validity:** Leo's independent geometry, horizon and link-model validation and visible uncertainty support the results.
- **Relevance:** the site/date flow and required basic comparison answer the lunar landing-site challenge.
- **Presentation:** the 30-second challenge → problem → app answer → significance storyboard demonstrates the decision.
- **Local jury (notes 14 Nov, not the official wording):** usefulness for researchers/astronauts → the one-sentence answer and basic comparison; intuitive use → UX-02 60-second run; innovation → sunlight, station line of sight and terrain in one flow; content over UI → validation table and the four mandatory site assessments.
